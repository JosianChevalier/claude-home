# principal-agent

The user's words: principal-agent theory is "the entire problem", the reason they still need to find a way of staying inside the loop. It is not for `philosophy/mandate.md`; it is to be used somewhere else, not yet chosen.

## What was told to the user, the material to place

Principal-agent theory (Jensen & Meckling, 1976; Holmström, 1979): a principal delegates to an agent that knows more than it does. Hidden information before the work, the agent's ability cannot be told; hidden action during it, what the agent did cannot be seen.

The cost of delegating, the agency costs, as read onto the harness:

- monitoring, the principal checks: the user rereading the work, context spent
- bonding, the agent proves itself: the report, the runs kaizen has made reliable
- residual loss, what still slips through: what was swept under the rug (φmandate)

The multitask result (Holmström & Milgrom, 1991): measure one part of a job and the agent neglects the rest; "make the tests pass" yields passing tests and a worse model.

The incentive half of the theory does not apply, a model has no interest to align; the information half does.

## Why the scale of automation does not describe the user's case

Sheridan & Verplank's ten levels of automation (1978) have one axis, and on it the machine always has the idea; the human approves, vetoes or is told. The user's case has the idea on the human side: the human tells, the machine acts, the human feels what comes back. The one-axis scale describes the harness only once the user steps out of the loop: a sub-agent, a workflow, autopilot; that is where the reachability dial of φmandate applies.

Who has drawn the user's case:

- Parasuraman, Sheridan & Wickens (2000), "A Model for Types and Levels of Human Interaction with Automation", IEEE Transactions on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics, Part A, 30(3), 286-297: automation rated per function, sensing, analysis, deciding, acting. The user is low on deciding, high on acting.
- Licklider (1960), "Man-Computer Symbiosis", IRE Transactions on Human Factors in Electronics, HFE-1, 4-11, and Engelbart's augmentation (1962): the human sets goals and judges, the machine does what can be routinised.
- Strong-style pairing (Falco), already in the user's rules.
- Supervisory control (Sheridan): near, but the human stands outside and watches.

Both papers are on the user's reading list.
