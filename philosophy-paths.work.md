# Philosophy paths

The README's Philosophy section: the schema and reading note (harness.md's figure), then page one and the paths a reader chooses from, a book where the reader is the hero. Readers include people who will never run an agent; the guide reads the README and offers the same paths, or any question.

Page one: `harness.md`. Each path carries one question into every file. Four scouts designed them, one per lens; step lines are their readings, to be checked against the files when written.

## The human

What does this do for my cognition, and why does it make my work with agents effective?

1. collaborative-production: a model of the work is built only by taking part, so the agent belongs inside the loop as driver.
2. transmission-belt: remove the medium's load, transmit the domain's, protect the germane; friction coming back is where understanding grows.
3. model-phenomenology: what I hold and the agent cannot, a type with a horizon; why the human stays central.
4. conversation-as-interface: the chat is my only medium, and I still go down to the files.
5. embodied-navigation: thinking runs on spatial machinery; moving through code is how I think with it.
6. mechanical-refactoring: moves cost no understanding, so understanding is spent only on the model.
7. batch-slicing: the step is the human's unit of thought; intent, not route.
8. compounding-decisions: decisions chain and weaken; the human is put on the ones that matter.
9. metis-kairos: what no level can absorb reaches me, and that arrival is the moment to improve.

Held at the end: I stay central because I hold what the agent cannot; the harness transmits the friction, and that is where I learn; every practice is sized to my attention.

Forks: after 4, zoom-levels (attention as the mirror of context economics); after 8, model-tiers (cognitive energy is sparser than tokens).

## The context

What does this do for the agent's context, and how does the harness's structure follow from it?

1. context-economics: context is scarce and degrades; all harness structure derives from this.
2. harness-elements: choosing an element is a loading decision first.
3. compounding-decisions: a full context is not only costly, it is weaker.
4. context-splitting: two axes, the sub-agent as the unit of both.
5. parse-dont-validate: the spatial cut made inside the code; a signature is a call site's whole context.
6. session-state-in-documents: the conversation is process memory, the file the backing store.
7. attractors: what a file does to a fresh context; it pulls, negation included.
8. collaborative-production: posture is upstream of context; the human in the loop is the shortest chain.
9. agentic-trunk-based-development: between agents, the diff and never the conversation.
10. agentic-extreme-programming: a lean context keeps the top model affordable, which keeps the context lean.

Held at the end: context is scarce and degrades, so the harness is shaped by what loads and when; the work is cut in space and time, and a file, never the conversation, crosses between contexts; the whole moves together.

Forks: at 2, three-layer-harness then harness-shape (loading policy by lifecycle); at 7, level-design or mechanical-refactoring (code is context).

## The practices

Which practice is this, what did it do for a team, what does it become applied to both sides?

1. agentic-extreme-programming: practices are complements; tokens, context, cognitive load, feedback move together.
2. batch-size-economics: holding cost is also cognitive load and context, so the optimum falls to a floor.
3. atomic-commit: the floor; validation, not generation, is the price.
4. mechanical-refactoring: the epistemic action moves to the agent; a cleaning pass is context hygiene.
5. trunk-based-development: commit = integrate = publish, the human-team form in full.
6. agentic-trunk-based-development: the same bound, a worktree as a committer; the purest before/after.
7. collaborative-production: mob programming and strong-style driving; the agent is the driver.
8. context-splitting: Beck's two hats and map-reduce applied to the context.
9. kairos-gates: a practice enters the loop as a step at its moment.
10. harness-direction: a practice's path from steered in session to script.

Held at the end: each known practice keeps its bound and changes its holder; the agent takes the mechanical part, the human keeps the decision that changes the concept; a practice enters as a gate and travels down to a script.

Forks: at 4, make-the-change-easy then change-in-place; at 8, parse-dont-validate then always-valid-domain, aggregates (the DDD entry).

## The whole

How do the human, the context and the loop hold together as one system?

1. collaborative-production: the loop is the boundary of one extended mind.
2. model-phenomenology: the engineer holds the type, the code the concept, the agent neither; no part stands in for another.
3. transmission-belt: one rule for all three parts; friction-feel is owned by no part.
4. abstraction-ladder: one shape; every ladder in the corpus is a sighting.
5. context-economics: one economy, two currencies, tokens and attention.
6. agentic-extreme-programming: integrity defined; feeling pulled between two sides is the test the set is not one.
7. compounding-decisions: waste in the human's currencies and reliability in the agent's are tied.
8. parse-dont-validate: one practice, three readings in the code itself.
9. embedded-reflexive: a breakdown climbs until the philosophy rewrites the engineer's own perception.

Held at the end: one extended mind, type in the engineer, concept in the code, transmission in the agent; one rule and one shape run across all; the system learns as one.

Forks: at 5, zoom-levels; at 8, mechanical-refactoring or agentic-trunk-based-development.
