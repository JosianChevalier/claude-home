# Writing

**Thesis.** A text is shaped for its reader, never in the shape of its input. A human reader is served drawn, the claim first, one decision at a time.

The three writing modes, human, agent and hybrid, and the agent's side: [[philosophy/level-design]].

## In general

The governing thought comes before its support (Minto, the pyramid principle): the reader decides at the top and dives only if the top is in doubt.

**Patchwriting** (Howard, composition studies): the source's structure kept, its words swapped, the text lightly rewritten. An agent does it by default. The input is the nearest basin in its context ([[philosophy/attractors]]): its order, its sections and its phrasing pull the output, whether the input is a pasted text, a file under review or the user's spoken words. It shows three ways:

- the text comes back in the input's order, reworded
- a list comes back a list, where its items belonged each in its own place
- a frame found in the input, a file's premise or a work file's open question, is taken as the question, untested

The counter is the one composition teaches: notes first, then the text written with the source closed. Every idea is extracted, tagged by where it goes (stays, merges, moves, cut) and reorganized; the new text is written from the reorganization. Below a paragraph the input's shape is rarely wrong, and the method costs more than it saves.

## Human

The human reads at capacity, through the conversation alone ([[philosophy/conversation-as-interface]]).

**The figure is the answer.** Abstract thought runs on the body's spatial machinery ([[philosophy/embodied-navigation]]). A figure is navigated with it: the eye goes to the box that matters, compares two columns, follows an arrow. Prose is decoded in one line, in the writer's order. So the figure carries the answer, and prose carries what a drawing cannot: an argument, a cause, a verdict, a choice.

The form follows the shape of the information, and each element earns its place by what it saves the reader, as ink does in Tufte's data-ink ratio. A form is recognised by its conventional mark, the header rule of a table, the bullets of a list, the arrows of a flow; without it, it is read as another. A figure carries the metaphor of what it draws, as image schemas do (Lakoff & Johnson): nested boxes say containment, an arrow says causation or time. Drawn from the word instead of the concept, it asserts a model nobody holds.

**The first mistake.** The human stops reading at the first mistake and corrects it, because the rest is usually built on it. Whatever follows a wrong first point is written for nobody: a turn opens with what decides, and holds one decision.

**The reread.** "tl;dr", "wall of text", the same point asked again: the message overflowed. The load was extraneous (Sweller), the form's, not the domain's ([[philosophy/zoom-levels]]). Explaining further adds to it; the answer is redone shorter and drawn.
