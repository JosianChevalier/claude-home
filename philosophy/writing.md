# Writing

**Thesis.** An agent writing from an input mirrors its structure: patchwriting. The ideas are cut loose from the input before anything is written.

## Patchwriting

**Patchwriting** (Howard, composition studies): the source's structure kept, its words swapped, the text lightly rewritten.

An agent does it by default. The input is the nearest basin in its context ([[philosophy/attractors]]): its order, its sections and its phrasing pull the output, whether the input is a pasted text, a file under review or the user's spoken words. It shows three ways:

- the text comes back in the input's order, reworded
- a list comes back a list, where its items belonged each in its own place
- a frame found in the input, a file's premise or a work file's open question, is taken as the question, untested

The counter is the one composition teaches: notes first, then the text written with the source closed. Every idea is extracted, tagged by where it goes (stays, merges, moves, cut) and reorganized; the new text is written from the reorganization. Cut loose from the input's shape, the ideas stop pulling toward it.

Below a paragraph the input's shape is rarely wrong, and the method costs more than it saves.
