# Level design

**Thesis.** Context engineering is level design: the writer shapes the landscape a reader moves through, and designs it from where the reader stands, not from where the writer stands.

## Two modes

Any actant, human or agent, is embedded, moving within a landscape, or reflexive, designing it. Writing a harness element is the reflexive mode: the designer asks what the player does and how they understand the world they move in. The failure is the dump, knowledge poured into a file from the writer's side.

The two modes stay connected: what the embedded side meets feeds the reflexive side as it goes ([[philosophy/transmission-belt]]).

## Designed whole

Every term in context is an asset placed in the landscape: a precise term brings a valley, a boundary a cliff or a fence ([[philosophy/attractors]]). The landscape is shaped as a whole. A fence does not block only the spot it stands on, and a term named to be avoided keeps its basin.

An attractor brought without ambiguity is an asset placed on purpose. Brought ambiguously, it is a random asset dropped in the middle of the terrain.

## The reader's landscape

The reader holds its weights and the context loaded for it, not the writer's. A concept the writer holds and the reader lacks is an obstacle: a term from a philosophy file the reader never loads, a word picked up earlier in the writer's session, a contradiction. The reader goes around it, then around the next, and eventually gets lost.

## Two costs

For an agent, the analogues of extraneous load (Sweller) and accidental complexity (Brooks):

- **Tokens**, the polygon count: the means.
- **Navigability**, the content: an easy stroll in the countryside, or broken terrain, obstacles everywhere, a walk across Mordor.

The measure is return per token. A schema returns little to an agent compared to text, but not less than nothing; an obstacle returns less than nothing. Sometimes more tokens are needed to build one coherent body of meaning.

## Embedded differs

A human is embedded through the senses, vision first, and cognition is embodied (Clark): they need a map, a level of detail and a sense of where they are ([[philosophy/zoom-levels]]). A human guided step by step through a procedure is a process to automate as early as possible.

An agent is embedded in semantics, read as a stream, not as a visual gestalt. Format counts where it carries meaning inline: a line break marks a new idea. A schema lowers a human's extraneous load and raises an agent's.

## Three writing modes

| Mode | Where | Designed for |
|---|---|---|
| Human | the chat | clarity above all: communication costs are asymmetric ([[philosophy/conversation-as-interface]]) |
| Agent | rules, skills, agents, work files, notebooks | the agent embedded |
| Hybrid | philosophy, plans, studies | the agent embedded, the human reflexive |

Agent text is written in attractors: it talks to the weights. What the weights or the preloaded context hold is not spelled out; what departs from the default is.

Hybrid text is consumed by the agent embedded, and skimmed, navigated, validated by the human reflexive, who dives into the detail only now and then. The text talks to the weights; the structure talks to the human.

## Probes

In the reflexive mode a human and an agent design together, in chat. Testing on that human is cheap: show and ask. Testing on another human is expensive. The agent struggles to take the place of a reader without its context, but testing that is cheap: a cognitive walkthrough (Wharton, Polson, Lewis) handed to a sub-agent of the type designed for, with nothing shared, asked what it understands.

Running the real task is rarely the cheaper test: in a complex domain, interactive with the human, it does not repeat and it costs the human too.

No probe is forced when the answer is obvious. One is run when a section is redesigned around an attractor in doubt.
