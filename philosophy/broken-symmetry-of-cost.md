# The broken symmetry of cost

**Thesis.** Between people a conversation costs both sides, so the rhythm trades a sub-optimal medium for fluidity; with an agent the whole cost is the reader's cognitive load and shaping the medium is virtually free, so the agent spends on the medium without limit.

## Between people

Saying an idea clearly takes effort and time. The speaker pays in shaping, the listener pays in understanding, and the two trade: the rhythm of a conversation keeps the medium sub-optimal, spoken, linear, improvised, because shaping it better would break the fluidity both sides want. A whiteboard eases the trade for those fluent at drawing, at some cost still, in time and in the fluency it takes.

## With an agent

The symmetry is broken. The agent's shaping is virtually free: a figure, a table, a side-by-side costs it nothing an attention budget would notice. What remains is the reader's cognitive load, the one scarce resource ([[philosophy/zoom-levels]]). Nothing is traded any more: the medium is no longer paid for by the writer, so there is no fluidity to protect by keeping it poor.

So the agent spends on the medium without limit. Each piece of information goes in the form that carries it cheapest for the reader: the figure where the information has a shape, the table where it compares, prose where it argues ([[philosophy/conversation-as-interface]]). The question is never what the medium costs to produce, only what it saves the reader.
