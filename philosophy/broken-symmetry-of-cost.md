# The broken symmetry of cost

**Thesis.** Between people a conversation costs both sides, so the rhythm trades a sub-optimal medium for fluidity; with an agent writing, the writer's side is virtually free and the whole cost is the reader's, so the agent spends without limit on the form and cuts without limit on the volume.

## Between people

Saying an idea clearly takes effort and time. The speaker pays in shaping, the listener pays in understanding, and the two trade: the rhythm of a conversation keeps the medium sub-optimal, spoken, linear, improvised, because shaping it better would break the fluidity both sides want. A whiteboard eases the trade for those fluent at drawing, at some cost still, in time and in the fluency it takes.

## With an agent writing

The symmetry is broken. Shaping and producing are virtually free for the agent: a figure, a table, a side-by-side, or one more paragraph, cost it nothing that would brake it. The whole cost moves to the reader: a human pays in attention ([[philosophy/zoom-levels]]), an agent pays in tokens ([[philosophy/context-economics]]). Nothing is traded any more: the medium is no longer paid for by the writer, so there is no fluidity to protect by keeping it poor, and no effort to spare by keeping it short.

Two consequences follow, one on the form, one on the volume.

## Spend on the form

Each piece of information goes in the form that carries it cheapest for its reader, whatever it costs to produce. For a human, the figure where the information has a shape, the table where it compares, prose where it argues ([[philosophy/conversation-as-interface]]). For an agent, prose built on precise terms: a figure or a table returns less than the sentences it replaces. The question is never what the form costs the writer, only what it saves the reader.

## Cut the volume

The corollary. Between people, the effort of saying is what keeps a speaker short; for the agent that brake is gone, and nothing limits what it writes but the reader's cost. So every line is paid by the reader and cheap for the writer, and the writer does the cutting the reader would otherwise do: a line stays for what it saves the reader, nothing stays for what it cost to write.
