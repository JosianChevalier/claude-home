# Types of constraints

**Thesis.** Snowden describes a constraint along several pairs, complementary rather than exclusive: governing or enabling, external or internal, containing or connecting, rigid, flexible or permeable; a constraint seen in its effect but not its cause is dark. Source: the EU field book on managing complexity and chaos in times of crisis (Snowden & Rancati, JRC).

| pair | one side | other side |
|---|---|---|
| governing / enabling | laws, rules, codes: stability, sensitive to change | heuristics, principles: guidance, decisions made locally |
| external / internal | exoskeleton: visible structure, limits growth | endoskeleton: self-similar, wider variety, fewer limits on growth |
| containing / connecting | categories, cells, departments: clear boundary conditions | links, networks: flexible and adaptive, at the cost of visibility and control |
| rigid / flexible / permeable | rigid: holds until its design conditions are exceeded, then breaks catastrophically | flexible: adapts to stress and constant change; permeable: lets exceptions through, at the risk of clogs |
| dark | the effect is seen, the cause is not | |

## Governing / enabling

The pair Cynefin is built on, from Juarrero's constraint-based reading of complexity: rigid constraints mark the clear domain, governing the complicated, enabling the complex, and no effective constraint the chaotic. Governing constraints are laws, rules and codes: they give stability and are sensitive to change. Enabling constraints are heuristics and principles: they guide while leaving the decision to whoever acts. Heuristics are mined from the narratives of what worked, then codified in memorable form with teaching stories; they work when compliance is measurable and they stay concrete, never as abstract platitudes.

## External / internal

An exoskeleton gives a clearly visible structure and limits how large its holder can grow; an endoskeleton makes its holders self-similar while allowing wider variety and fewer limits on growth. Organisation design builds the internal one as scaffolding and points of coherence around which units interact: ritualised meetings, evaluations. External constraints are the ones set from outside: markets, resources, social foundations, environmental ceilings.

## Containing / connecting

Containers, like categories, cells or departments, give clear, reassuring boundary conditions. Connections, like links in a network, give a flexible, adaptive structure, paid in visibility and control. Changing connections costs less than restructuring containers; as new connections prove useful, they are tightened and formalised into new units.

## Rigid / flexible / permeable

A rigid constraint resists until its design conditions are exceeded, then breaks catastrophically; a deadline is meant rigid. A flexible one adapts to stress and constant change; flexi-time is one. Permeability, special conditions that let exceptions through, adds resilience to either, at the risk of clogs: too many applying for the exception.

## Dark constraints

Named after dark matter: the effect is seen, the cause is not. A term carrying different meanings for different people is one: it is named, and the reactions differ. Narratives are their antidote. The share of the past the known constraints explain measures the risk ahead: the less they explain, the less can be monitored, and the likelier a catastrophic surprise.
