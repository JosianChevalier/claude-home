# UX

## Doctrine

- The screen is an expression of the model, the view exposed to the user: its words are the ubiquitous language (Evans), its actions the model's operations.
- Two axes, DDD's. Strategic: which models the screen exposes, and in whose words; the conceptual model comes before the screen (Johnson & Henderson), the objects before the actions and the layout (Prater). Tactical: how that view is expressed in the grammar of human cognition. Model before grammar: no law repairs a screen that says the wrong concept.
- The tactical axis stands on Yablonski's Laws of UX: a law is used under his name for it and as he states it, and his site, lawsofux.com, settles any doubt on what a law says.
- Structure goes to cognition before it goes to the screen: a group is shown by proximity before a box, an action by its convention before a label. A limit of cognition is read as a lever. What memory would have to carry goes on the screen (Norman).
- Task-based UI (Young): the screen offers the model's operations, each named as the model names it, and sends the intent. A CRUD form over a model that has behaviour loses the reason for the change.
- An invariant shows as an absent affordance, never a disabled control or an error after the fact.
- Complexity is conserved (Tesler): what the model does not absorb, the user carries.

## Know-how

- A law is named at the decision it settles, in parentheses, never explained. The laws are heuristics, never a checklist.
- A screen that feels wrong is a model question first: a concept the model does not hold, a word that is not the model's, the user's model against ours. A grammar question only after.
- The laws in space, the structure of one screen: Proximity, Similarity, Common Region, Uniform Connectedness, Prägnanz, Von Restorff, Fitts, Hick, Choice Overload, Miller, Chunking, Working Memory, Cognitive Load, Selective Attention.
- The laws in time, the structure of a process, where the model's lifecycle shows: Serial Position, Peak-End, Goal-Gradient, Zeigarnik, Flow, Doherty. What is unfinished is shown, and the user carries where they are.
- The laws at the boundary between the user's model and ours: Jakob, Mental Model, Tesler, Postel, the Paradox of the Active User. The user learns the model by acting, so the screen teaches in the act; loose input is parsed into the model's types.
- One screen, one task, and its title states it (Microsoft's inductive user interface).
- Aesthetic-Usability is a hazard in a test: a pleasing screen hides its faults.
